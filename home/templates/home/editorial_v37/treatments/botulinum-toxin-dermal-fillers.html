01 / Two different tools

Movement and volume are not the same problem.

Botulinum toxin and fillers differ in mechanism, goals, duration and risk profile. Suitability starts with the actual anatomy and concern.

Botulinum toxin

Selected muscle activity.

Temporarily reduces activity in selected muscles. Cosmetic use may soften movement-related lines while retaining appropriate facial movement according to the treatment plan.

Dermal fillers

Selected volume and contour.

Filler adds or restores volume in selected facial areas. Product, tissue plane, anatomy and treatment goal all matter.

Not interchangeable

One does not simply replace the other.

Some patients may be suitable for one, both or neither after assessment of movement, structure and risk.

02 / Assessment

The face should be assessed at rest and in motion.

A good injectable consultation is not a preset injection map. Facial movement, symmetry, proportions, previous treatment, skin condition, medical history and desired amount of change influence planning.

03 / Natural expression

“Frozen” should not be the goal by default.

Botulinum toxin is used to reduce selected muscle activity. Treatment area, dose and injection points depend on anatomy, muscle strength and how much movement the patient wants to retain.

04 / Filler planning

Treat the face—not one isolated line.

Whole-face assessment considers bone structure, soft-tissue volume, skin quality, lip shape, chin or jaw balance, prior filler and relevant medical or dental history.

05 / FAQ

Questions worth asking before injectables.

Are botulinum toxin and fillers the same?
No. Botulinum toxin reduces selected muscle activity. Fillers add or restore volume. Some patients may be suitable for one, both or neither.
Does botulinum toxin add volume?
No. It does not replace facial volume; it acts on selected muscles.
Should every person get the same injection map?
No. Facial anatomy, muscle strength, asymmetry, medical history and desired movement vary between people.
Can fillers copy a filtered photograph?
That should not be the planning goal. Suitability should follow real facial anatomy, proportions, safety and realistic expectations.
Are injectables risk-free or completely painless?
No medical injectable should be presented as risk-free or guaranteed painless. Expected discomfort, common side effects, less common complications and aftercare should be discussed during consent.
