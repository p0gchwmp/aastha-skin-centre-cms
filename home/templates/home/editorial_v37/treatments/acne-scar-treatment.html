01 / Scar morphology

Texture is not one diagnosis.

Dark or red post-acne marks can exist without a permanent textural scar. True scars may be narrow and deep, broad and shallow, tethered or raised.

Rolling scars

Broad, shallow depressions can create a wavy surface. Some are tethered by deeper fibrous bands, which changes procedural planning.

02 / Before treatment

Active acne usually comes first.

Continuing breakouts can create new scars while older scars are being treated. Scar work is commonly planned after acne is reasonably controlled.

03 / Treatment planning

Different tools solve different structural problems.

Possible options in Aastha’s pathway include MNRF, microneedling, subcision, fractional CO₂ laser, focal techniques, selected peels for associated pigmentation and combination plans.

Treatment logic

Release tethering

When fibrous bands pull selected scars downward, subcision may be considered to release deeper tethering.

05 / Practical questions

Before you commit to a scar plan.

Can one session remove acne scars?
Acne scars vary in type and severity. Improvement is usually staged, and multiple sessions or combined methods may be recommended. Complete removal in one session should not be promised.
Are acne marks the same as acne scars?
No. Flat red, brown, grey-brown or purple marks can be post-inflammatory colour changes without a permanent change in skin texture.
Why might more than one procedure be recommended?
Mixed scar patterns are common. A technique that helps tethering may not address a narrow deep scar, while resurfacing may not solve every structural problem.
When should scar treatment begin?
It commonly begins after active acne is reasonably controlled, while acne maintenance may continue during the scar-treatment course.
