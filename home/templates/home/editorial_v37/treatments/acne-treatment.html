01 / What acne is

More than “just pimples.”

Acne is an inflammatory condition involving blocked hair follicles, oil production and inflammation. It may affect the face, chest, shoulders and back and can leave red marks, dark marks or permanent textural scars.

Treatment aim

Control active breakouts.

Reduce existing acne, prevent new lesions and lower the chance of permanent scarring.

Age

Teenage or adult.

Acne can persist into adulthood or begin for the first time later in life.

Look-alikes

Not every bump is acne.

Folliculitis, perioral dermatitis and other acne-like eruptions can need different treatment.

Comedonal acne

Blackheads and whiteheads form when follicles become blocked. Planning usually focuses on preventing new blockages and controlling accompanying inflammation.

03 / Assessment

What gets checked before treatment.

A good acne plan begins with pattern, severity, scarring risk and context—not a fixed package.

Lesion pattern
Blackheads, whiteheads, inflammatory lesions, nodules or cysts and where they occur.
Severity
How active and widespread the acne is and whether marks or scars are developing.
Previous treatment
Medicines, skincare, response, irritation and why earlier treatment may have been stopped.
Medical context
Current medicines, medical history and menstrual or hormonal symptoms when relevant.
Testing
Tests are not required for every patient. Selected investigations may be considered when history or examination suggests an underlying issue.
04 / Treatment route

A treatment plan should have an order.

There is no single acne treatment suitable for everyone. Acne type, severity, body area, age, pregnancy considerations, previous response and scar tendency all matter.

Treatment logic

Confirm the diagnosis

Acne-like eruptions can resemble acne but need different treatment, so examination matters before escalation.

01 / Confirm

Make sure it really is acne.

Similar-looking eruptions can follow a different treatment pathway.

02 / Assess

Severity changes the plan.

Deeper inflammatory acne and rapid scarring deserve different urgency than a few comedones.

03 / Control

Active acne usually comes first.

Treatment is selected for the diagnosis, severity and individual patient rather than a universal routine.

04 / Residual concerns

Marks and scars are different problems.

Colour and texture may need separate but coordinated treatment.

05 / Maintain

Good control is not always one-and-done.

Follow-up allows the plan to be adjusted to response and tolerability.

05 / When to come in

Some acne deserves earlier assessment.

  • Painful deep lesions or rapid scarring
  • Persistent dark or red marks
  • Repeated recurrence despite treatment
  • Extensive face, chest or back involvement
  • Sudden adult-onset acne
  • Possible hormonal symptoms
  • Significant impact on confidence or daily life
  • A rash that does not look like typical acne
  • Uncertainty about medicines or skincare
06 / Practical questions

Before you change your routine.

Is acne caused by poor hygiene?
No. Acne is not simply a hygiene problem, and aggressive scrubbing can irritate inflamed skin.
Should acne marks and scars be treated the same way?
No. Flat colour changes and permanent textural scars are different problems. Active acne is commonly controlled before scar-specific procedures are planned.
Does every patient need tests?
No. Investigations are selected when the history or examination suggests they could change diagnosis or management.
